# Are Electric Cars Fun to Drive?

It's the question I have been asking myself for years. As a true petrol head for me it was always about the exaggerated drama around the cars, starting from their looks, finishing with the sound of the petrol engine. At the time of writing this post I own no car nor motorcycle, though my last car was Tesla Model 3 Performance from 2020, and prior to that I have owned over 20 different cars and motorcycles, some of them were just regular cars like Ford Fiesta, others had a bit of a soul and uniqueness like Mazda MX-5 Miata, Honda CRX, or BMW 435i M-Performance. The past year I spent with my first electric car, and I was a complete stereotype breaker, as I didn't even have a charging plug at home, and I used the car mostly on the highways.

# Prelude

Since I can remember, my first toys were small cars, my early childhood from the age of around 4 till my teenage years, was about riding my bicycle - the thing that gave me freedom as a kid, and probably even too much of it. When I was walking on the street with my mother, I kept asking her what is the brand name and model of the car that caught my attention. The world of the 90s was a very different place, life seemed to be much calmer (at least from the perspective of a kid) and people didn't have so many options as they do have now. Growing up in Poland in the 90s probably also added to this the fact that people were interested in having foreign cars, as these were the first times in decades that many people were finally able to own them. When I look back at the 90s, things that come to my mind are definitely cars, sport bikes, music, movies, and the early hype around computers and the Internet - which I feel like most people didn't even take seriously. Those were also the times when we had much more limited content, so we had to use our imagination. Of course, those were also the years of Formula 1, Rallies, and first video games.

# The Peak

It was probably somewhere around the year 2000, mid-afternoon, my mother picked me up from the rehab center, and we stopped at an electronics shop. My attention was taken by a huge TV. There was a police officer (becoming a police officer was my dream at that time, how cool would it be to catch the bad guys and be like one of those movie heroes?) who had a sort of double life. By day he upheld the law, but by night he was racing a car. All of a sudden, they are in the middle of the street, racing exotic cars I had never seen before, one of the guys even had PS1 in his car, and the main character used a laptop during the race to boost performance on the fly. You have probably already guessed what film was that - it was of course Fast & Furious. When the race was over, and the Yakuza guys destroyed Brian's Mitsubishi Eclipse, my mother came back and took me out of this dream world. From that moment on, I have no idea what happened. I kept thinking about the movie over and over. Even as a kid I had these moments of grayness, and questioning life. It was the time before the wide-spread Internet and social media, so of course I was getting bored quite often. On top of it there was once again the reality of growing up in Poland of those days. However, when I saw an interesting car passing by on the street - those were the moments when nothing else mattered for a while. Briefly, life had some light and colour to it, I looked at these cars, and appreciated their beauty, sound, and drama around it.

# Growing up

In my childhood and teenage years, I could categorise those moments of light in life and being genuinely inspired by something probably by 3 categories: automotive, music, and technology. The special spiritual feeling, when you hear the song, and it gives you heavy nostalgia, or the sensation that you cannot even describe because of something that is not truly physical, the intrigue of seeing new video games, and thinking how could they recreate the graphics or the physics so accurately, being taken by the plot of the story, or experiencing the beauty of what can be built by humans, and how human can unite with the machine and push it to its limits - sometimes even putting your own life at risk.

When I entered my teenage years, I could say that one of the iconic games for me were arcade racing games - NFS Underground, and later on simulators such as Live for Speed, or Richard Burns Rally. When I watched TV, I only tuned into Top Gear or Monster Garage. Naturally, I was getting interested not only in the cars themselves but also in the wider spectrum such as mechanics, and what kind of emotions they bring to people. In those years before I turned 18, I also spent countless hours fixing my bicycle in the basement, as well as my nitro RC cars. The closest I got to real cars, was around my 16th birthday, when I attended car rally for the first time.

# The Culture

By the time I was at high-school, driving my first* car (Honda Civic), I was also surrounded by people who loved cars. Most of the weekends I would spend either being at rally, organising one, or attending a car meetup. In those days I met a lot of people, who remain my close friends to this day. What connected us, was the passion for cars, the emotions they give and exchanging the knowledge of how to improve or fix something. However, on the flip side, it had its dark side too. Starting with burning through money that could have been spent on saving for own apartment, investing into own business, education etc. Going further, with not always obeying the law, like drifting in a public car park (we generally tried to do it at night, when nobody was around), to putting our life and health in danger - luckily nobody in my close circle ever got injured in accident or anything like that. The cars of those times, were also not perfect - often we could afford only old imported cars, that had their "golden years" already long gone and many things were falling apart. Lastly, there were also plenty of people, who I feel like might have not shared the passion with us, but rather wanted to be seen or create some sort of persona around them thanks to the car. On top of it, we could also add environmental dangers, like pollution etc. Being fully aware of all of the downsides of the car culture, still it was something that connected people for a greater good. Many teenagers and young adults tend to go down the drugs, parties, and alcohol path those days, which ended tragically in many cases.

# The Transition

Around mid 2010s, when I was a university student, studying abroad, in the country where cars were much more expensive and no one could afford a car during their studies, the shift started to happen. Slowly, the sales of sports cars were not as high as before. The interest was not there anymore, and I felt like the innovation that cars used to bring was also gone, or at least not the feeling of having a sports car. People became more aware of the environmental crisis, and the whole industry started changing. Something started to fade away, something that defined my childhood.

# New Era

Back then, there used to be lots of debates about whether we should move away from internal combustion engines and switch to electric cars. Naturally, I was quite opposed to the idea, and my heart was bleeding just with the idea of cars without a soul, as for me the poster child of the electric car was Toyota Prius - definitely not the car that brought any emotion, and most importantly it didn't make any sound. At the same time, there was a huge shift in the accessibility to technology. Just in a couple of years, most of the people around me owned a smartphone, and Internet became a new normal. As I was interested in technology and computers probably equally as much as cars since my childhood - I also got interested in the phenomenon of Tesla and SpaceX.

# Different Perspective

Internal combustion engines were something I could talk about for hours. The position of the pistons, valve system, intake system, and last but not least the sound that gives character to each of them. Some engines like V8 have a better torque in the lower part of the rpm, while giving it the unique low frequency sound. Honda B and D series engines, with variable valve system and the direct injection system that gave these engines the additional boost in the higher rpm range and the iconic over 9000 meme that referred to Honda B Series engines revving so high. Now, with the introduction of electric motors - it all was gone, no more thrill. Yes, from one side - simplicity of the electric driving unit, and flat torque that could smash most of the road cars in most of the conditions, but on the other hand nothing to tune up, no sound, no emotion, no story to be told, perfect to the degree of boredom. 

# So Why Tesla Then?

Perfection lies in the imperfection? Maybe that's why we still love going to concerts to listen to raw, unprocessed versions of our favorite songs, instead of the perfect studio version. There is something deeply human about having flaws that define us, the art and most of the things around us. The issue with electric cars is that they solve most of the combustion engine's problems to the level of near perfection.

So how did I end up buying electric myself? Despite my pessimistic view on the electric cars, I still wanted to experience one and see how it actually is in practice. Over a year ago, after owning Mazda MX-5, I realised that I needed a car that would be a little bit more practical, and at the same time newer. I wanted to buy something that would be maximum few years old, but still come with warranty, and something that wouldn't cost a fortune, that would be durable, while still being fun. No matter how I measured it, it looked like Tesla Model 3 Performance, with the prices dipping in some cases below 14k euro, were the cars that were only a few years old, most of them still had warranty, plus the Performance edition that comes with nearly 500 horse-power, track mode, and a bit over 3 seconds to 100kmh would be the best choice. In a similar price range I could get cars like Toyota Yaris (the regular one not GR ;) or Hyundai I30N. After spending so much money over the years on parts and fuel, I finally wanted something economical, relatively cheap, but still fun. The whole irony of this situation is that I ended up getting a real monster, and definitely the most powerful and the fastest vehicle (including motorcycles) I ever owned.

# The Bad and The Ugly

Even though electric cars solve most of the internal combustion cars flaws, they also introduce different types of drawbacks. The main one is the range, and the second one is weight. Let's start from the range then. On the Internet you can find various WLTP values for the range of such cars. In the last year I have met quite a lot of people who owned Teslas and other electric cars, and I also owned one for a year. While most of the petrol cars nowadays can give you from 300 up to 1000 km of range per single tank, with the refuelling possible within 5 mins, the EVs, or most of the ones I had a chance to drive could make 150-350km per one charge. My Performance was the one with probably the worst possible range, but still I could say that in most of the scenarios the range difference compared to other variants was only around 50km, so not that much I would say.

300 kilometers - not that bad you may say, that was my initial thought at least. But to achieve this kind of range I had to drive on the highway during spring, with my AC and heater off, possibly most of the time below 90 km/h. More often than not it was closer to 200-250 km, and in the winter it was going down to as little as 150 km. Still, if you have a charger at home it's kind of acceptable, but in my case the closest Tesla charger was 20 kilometers away. All of this lowered the usable range by another 50 kilometers, as I didn't want to end up without a charge.

Another related issue are the chargers themselves, by now there are a lot of charging stations everywhere. However, not every charger supports fast charging, some of them have a very high rate which makes charging sometimes more expensive than driving a petrol car, and some were broken. Additionally, there were queues sometimes, and often the chargers required their own dedicated app to connect and register a payment card.

Charging is not always as slow as people think - while home charging can take up to 20 hours, most modern fast chargers could charge my car in about 30-40 minutes. At first, I didn't mind, especially since Tesla rates were relatively low, making the cost of covering 100 km similar to owning a car that would consume around 5 liters of gasoline. If you take into consideration that we are driving heavy car with 500 hp, it looks like a dream. In reality though, I ended up sometimes doing the 2-hour drive in about 4 hours, as I had to charge at the beginning and at the end. What's more, the fast and cheap chargers were not always conveniently placed, so there were cases when I had to leave the main road of my trip, add up around 20 kms to recharge the car.

OK, but anyway after 2-3 hrs it's good to take a rest. Yes, it's true, on long trips especially, when I was doing my Europe road trip, sometimes covering 1000 km in a day, it was really nice to have these kinds of longer breaks when I could rest, eat something and have a coffee. However, for the shorter trips, or simply using the car in the city - it was just a pure waste of time and additional commitment, just to keep the car charged.

These were the things, that I definitely consider as a genuine flaw that it's hard to find any positive sides of these. For the rest of the shortcomings of Tesla Model 3 Performance 2020 in particular, I would say it's somewhere in the middle. One of these is the weight - well generally the less the better, in the case of EVs the added weight at the bottom of the car can indeed have its own upsides such as a lower center of gravity and as a result better handling. Let me start from the good one - the car had almost no body roll at all. Even on not the best stock performance suspension, it was very hard to feel any body roll, the traction was more than satisfactory for a machine that weighs over 1800 kg and it's still one of the lightest EVs in its class. On the other hand, I personally found that the weight really affected the braking distance. At least at the stock performance brakes, I have experienced many cases when I had my foot fully on the brake, and I felt like ZERO extra margin to recover in case I needed to brake a bit harder.

On a similar note is the steering wheel. From one side it was relatively small, and lock to lock was barely one full turn which puts the car in the territory of serious rally cars. However, it felt completely detached from the car, I am not sure how exactly it works, but the feeling was as if it was fully electric, just sending signals to the motor that turns the wheel. I would say it was like playing a video game on a race sim, but it wasn't enough, as you tend to have way more feedback in games than in Tesla. The only feedback I experienced was the simulated vibration while driving over the lanes. I even took the car to the race track, and despite having drift mode, it wouldn't counter-steer when you let go. The only way, is to use your muscle memory and counter-steer manually. No wonder why people install wisefab BMW steering racks for drifting.

# Lack of Spirit Inside

If you have ever driven a sport version of a regular street car, like for example BMW M you will find different seats, steering wheel, and accents that make you aware that you sit in a very special edition of the car. At least 2020 model Tesla Model 3 Performance, completely lacks this feature. The interior looks identical to the regular Model 3. All this connected with the fact that we are driving an electric car, was heartbreaking, as there was no spark of additional emotion, and completely zero awareness that we are sitting in the car that has nearly 500 hp. Maybe it's just a small detail, though for me it was also about these details that were adding up to the spirit of the sports cars. Speaking of the cabin, it also deserves its own place.

Yes, the car has zero traditional gauges - there is only one big tablet in the middle and that's all. Despite my whole criticism above, I kind of like it. The minimalistic approach left much more space and also gave me a sense of "connection" to the car, rather than being distracted by gazillions of buttons. However, once again what seemed to be an advantage at first sight, turned out to make things even more complex, and even some super simple tasks like opening the front box required going through the menu or using voice control. The lack of the instrument cluster in the middle wasn't also that bad, though I wouldn't mind having BMW styled HUD on the windshield. To finish it with something positive, I have to say that the cabin quality and audio (if it counts as interior) was one of the best in its price-quality ratio. Sure, you can feel the leather is synthetic, and there is some squeaking from time to time here and there like in most other cars, but overall most of the parts are quite soft in touch and it doesn't leave the impression of being a super cheap car with low quality hard plastics (even though you can find them there as well in a few places).

# Would I Buy It Again?

Well, probably not. It was still, most likely the best car I ever had, especially in this price-quality ratio, though the main thing I remember while thinking back on it, is the time I spent charging it, not the 3 seconds thrill of acceleration. I still think that electric cars are the future, they solve most of the disadvantages of petrol engines, and they don't give this peculiar feeling of being worn out while driving them - internal combustion engines approximately give only about 30% of pure driving power, rest is heat, so it's rather a side effect than the core. Ironically, electric cars like the Tesla Model 3 Performance give the driver more direct connection to the car, where you are not distracted by so many processes that happen in between like the boost pressure loading or gear change. Yet, once again - wasn't it all a bit more about the whole drama around it? The feeling of being in control, changing character of each car with each driver creating a unique combo. 

How about you? Do you think that it's just a transition period or are we going to see the great comeback of the good old automotive world? Looking at the current situation, I feel like the whole hype about cars is gone. There are still people who genuinely like cars, not only for show-off but they really share the passion, even among the youngsters. However, my feeling is that with the new generation it becomes more of a niche rather than something general. Even Tesla itself as far as I know abandoned the development of new Roadster model, in favour of creating Robotaxi. Nevertheless, without going into politics, Tesla definitely showed that electric cars can also offer something interesting for car lovers, as well as set a brand new trend in the automotive world where impossible became possible, not just by producing exciting cars but also by building a completely new automotive brand from scratch within years and achieving global recognition.

The electric cars definitely have something to offer, even to real petrol-heads, as ironically you can be much more connected to the car itself. Cars like Tesla Model 3 Performance, offer not only great acceleration, but decent handling, track mode, and ability to play a bit with the way the car handles. Still, it all comes with a price and at least I feel like the foundation of why we loved cars so much, why there were a bit like us is fading away. Of course there are some companies like Hyundai with it's Ioniq 5 and 6 N series that not only emit a fake petrol engine sound, but also mimic the gearbox. Looking at the trend, it may be that it's just a transition period for the people who still remember the past, but the main focus is to make the future of mobility electric and autonomous - maybe even owning a car will be weird in a couple of years. We could also argue, that most likely people over 100 years ago where saying the same - my great grandmother's brother had his own horses and he loved them for the "soul" and emotions they had. It's not even 100 years ago, when people were using animals to travel around. Why wouldn't this whole cars stuff be just a temporary thing of our times? It's a bit sad, but at the same time if we hold on too much to the present, we cannot look fully into the present. What are your thoughts on it?
